The Katy and Houston metro area has no shortage of paved trails — these are the parks I keep coming back to for inline skating and cycling. Some are smooth enough for speed work on the **EdgeTek**; others are rougher but were close enough to hit every day. Your mileage (and wheel wear) may vary.

## At a glance

| Park | Activity | Surface / route | Notes |
| ---- | -------- | --------------- | ----- |
| John Paul Landing Park | Skating | — | **FR1** and **EdgeTek** |
| Bear Creek Park | Skating | Loop to soccer fields; Clay to Patterson | Regular loop route |
| Terry Hershey Park | Cycling & skating | West section (cycling); east toward Beltway 8 (skating) | Split use by section |
| George Bush Park | Cycling (via TH) | Through Terry Hershey and back | ~**40 miles** round trip |
| Cullen Park Trail | Skating & biking | Rough for skates; better on the bike | Daily (sometimes twice) when it was close |

## John Paul Landing Park

Skating on both the **FR1** and **EdgeTek** — a solid spot when I want pavement and room to roll on different setups.

## Bear Creek Park

My usual loop runs out to the **soccer fields**, with **Clay to Patterson** as part of the route. A dependable Bear Creek session when I want a familiar loop without leaving the west side.

## Terry Hershey Park

I split the park by section: **west** for **cycling**, **east toward Beltway 8** for **skating**. Long, popular bayou corridor — different vibes and surfaces depending on which end you start from.

## George Bush Park

Often tied into a bigger day: through **Terry Hershey Park and back** is about **40 miles** — a serious endurance ride when the legs (and schedule) allow.

## Cullen Park Trail

The trail surface **sucks for skates**, but it's where I skated the **most** — **every day**, sometimes **twice a day**, because it was so close. Chewed up my wheels; sometimes had to **jump fallen branches**. **Biking** is better on the **Scorpion**; the **V20** is solid here too.
